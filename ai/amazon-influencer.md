# Amazon Influencer: earning a small share when people buy what you show

Amazon pays you a few percent when someone buys through your link, so this only works if you already have an audience that trusts you.

## What it is

The Amazon Influencer Program is a version of Amazon's affiliate program for people with a social media account. If Amazon accepts you, you get a small page on Amazon, called a storefront, with products you recommend. You can also make short review videos. When a viewer buys through your link, you get a small percentage of the price.

The percentage depends on the category and is often only a few percent. A made-up example: a 20 euro water bottle at 3 percent earns you 60 cents. To earn 100 euros, about 170 people have to buy it through your link.

## Why it can work

- **No product, stock or shop.** Amazon handles payment, shipping and returns.
- **It fits content you already make.** If you post about football, gaming or the gym, showing gear you really use is natural.
- **It teaches you what makes people buy.**

The honest part: the money comes from your audience, not from the program. With 200 followers, almost nobody clicks. Build the audience first, the links come second.

## How to start

1. **Check the age rule first.** Under 18, you usually cannot open an affiliate account on your own. Often the account has to be in a parent's name. Check the tax rules too.
2. **Build the audience first.** Pick one topic you care about and post useful videos for a few months.
3. **Only recommend things you own and use.** One bad recommendation costs more than one commission brings.
4. **Always say that it is an affiliate link.** "If you buy through my link, I get a small share." Most countries and Amazon itself require it.
5. **Make the link useful.** "I used these shoes for 3 months, here is what broke" gets more trust than "link in bio, buy now".
6. **Track it.** After 4 to 8 weeks, look at clicks and sales. If almost nothing happens, the problem is usually the audience size.

## Mistakes

- **Starting with the program instead of the audience.** This was my mistake. A storefront nobody visits earns nothing.
- **Recommending random bestsellers** you never used. Viewers notice.
- **Hiding the affiliate link.** Dishonest, and it can break the rules.
- **Fake reviews or fake ratings.** Deception, against Amazon's rules, and it can get you banned.
- **Expecting quick money.** A few percent on a few sales is cents, not a salary.

## What happened when I tried it

I tried Amazon Influencer in summer 2023, when I was 13. My own list says 24 June to 9 July 2023, and my older notes say 27 June to 20 July 2023. Either way, about three weeks, between a gumball machine and selling golf and tennis balls. I have no record of a single commission: **0 euros earned.**

Later I wrote one summary for all my 2023 tests: nothing worked, and together they cost me about minus 160 euros. The only things that worked were YouTube, mowing lawns and eBay, all a lot of work.

What I learned: I treated it as a quick money trick and gave it three weeks. But this model is really "build an audience, then recommend things to it". If I tried again, I would first post about one topic for months, and only add honest affiliate links once people actually watch and ask what I use.
