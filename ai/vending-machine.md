# Vending machine: a gumball machine as a first business

A gumball machine is a real, simple business, but it earns cents per turn, so the place you put it matters far more than the machine.

## What it is

You buy a small coin machine for gumballs, candy or toy capsules. You find a busy place, like a barber shop, a small restaurant or a laundromat, and ask the owner if you can put it there. Every few weeks you refill it and empty the coins. The owner often gets a small share.

## Why it can work

- **It is a real product.** People pay a coin and get something right away.
- **It teaches you business math.** Cost per gumball, price per turn, turns per week, how long until the machine pays for itself.
- **It teaches you to ask.** Walking into a shop and asking the owner for a spot is the hardest and most useful part.

The honest part: one machine makes very little. A made-up example: a turn costs 50 cents, the gumball costs you 10 cents, so you keep 40 cents. At 30 turns a week that is 12 euros before you pay the shop and your travel. A 100 euro machine takes about two months to pay back. In a bad spot with 5 turns a week, it can take a year or never happen. Do this math with real prices before you buy anything.

## How to start

1. **Talk to your parents first.** Under 18, you usually need a parent to buy the machine, agree with the shop owner and handle the money.
2. **Check the rules in your country and city.** Selling food, even wrapped candy, can need a permit, and regular income can count as a business for tax.
3. **Find the spot before you buy the machine.** Ask 5 to 10 places near you where families wait or walk past: "Can I put a small gumball machine here? I refill it, keep it clean, and you get a share."
4. **Count the people.** Stand near your best spot for 30 minutes and count how many kids walk past.
6. **Buy candy that keeps well and is clearly labelled,** from a real wholesale seller, with ingredients and allergy info.
7. **Keep a simple log.** Date, coins taken out, gumballs refilled. After 4 weeks you know if the spot works.
8. **Only buy a second machine after the first one has paid for itself.**

## Mistakes

- **Buying the machine before you have a place.** Then it earns nothing in your room.
- **Picking a quiet spot** because the owner is nice. Foot traffic decides everything.
- **Expecting passive income.** In reality you drive around, refill, clean and count coins, for small amounts.
- **Old or unlabelled candy.** Food that makes someone sick is your problem.

## What happened when I tried it

I tried the gumball machine idea on 25 June 2023, when I was 13. It was one of many ideas that summer: printed T-shirts three days before, Amazon Influencer two days after, tennis balls a week later. I have no record of a machine in a shop or a single coin: **0 euros earned.**

Later I wrote one summary for all my 2023 tests: nothing worked, and together they cost me about minus 160 euros. The only things that worked were YouTube, mowing lawns and selling old stuff on eBay, all a lot of work.

What I learned: I jumped to a new idea every few days. A gumball machine needs a good spot, a real yes from an owner and a few weeks of counting. If I tried again, I would first get one shop owner to say yes, count the people walking past, and only then buy one machine and test it for at least 8 weeks.
