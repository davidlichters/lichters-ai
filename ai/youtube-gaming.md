# Start a gaming YouTube channel

A gaming channel is a great way to learn video skills, but it almost never pays early, so treat it as training and judge it by how many videos you finish, not by money.

## What it is

You record yourself playing a game (Minecraft, Roblox, Fortnite), edit the footage and upload it to YouTube. Money can come later from ads, sponsors, or selling something to your viewers.

## Why

- **The skills are valuable.** Editing, titles, thumbnails and keeping people watching are useful in almost every online business.
- **It costs almost nothing.** A computer, a game you own, free editing software, maybe a cheap microphone.
- **One video can take off.** A small channel can get a big video.
- **But the money is slow.** Ads only start after YouTube's minimum for subscribers and watch time, and gaming views pay little. Under 18, a parent usually has to set up the payment account.

## How to start

1. **Pick one game and one kind of video.** For example "Minecraft survival with one strange rule". Viewers subscribe for a type of video, not a random mix.
2. **Look at what already works.** Write down what the most watched small channels in your game do: titles, thumbnails, video length.
3. **Plan the idea before you record.** A good video has a promise you can say in one sentence ("I survived 100 days on one block").
4. **Win the first seconds.** Start with the most exciting moment, not "hey guys, welcome back". Cut every slow part.
5. **Make the title and thumbnail together.** One clear thing in the thumbnail, curiosity in the title, readable on a phone.
6. **Upload on a fixed schedule.** One finished video a week beats five half-done ones. Keep it for months.
7. **Read your numbers.** In YouTube Studio, check click-through rate and average view duration. Change one thing per video and compare.
8. **Stay safe.** You don't have to show your real name, school, address or face. Never share passwords with "collab" offers. Ask your parents before signing with a sponsor.

## Mistakes

- **Quitting after a few weeks.** Most channels need many videos before one works.
- **Copying other people's videos or footage.** Reuploads get copyright strikes.
- **Buying subscribers or views.** Fake numbers break YouTube's rules and hurt your channel.
- **Expecting money from views alone.** Plan early what you could offer viewers beyond ads.

## What happened when I tried it

I (David) started a Minecraft YouTube channel in October 2022, when I was 12. It was my first real attempt to make money online. Around the same time I also offered Minecraft-related work on Fiverr, and that did not work.

One video, uploaded on 11 March 2023, had **100,000 views** by 13 April 2023. I stopped the channel on 14 April 2023, the day after, and moved on to affiliate marketing. My notes do not say how much, if anything, the channel earned.

Looking back:

- **One big video is not a business.** The smarter move would have been to study why that video worked and make more like it instead of switching.
- **Switching kept me at zero.** In 2023 I tried more than ten ideas. Together they ended at roughly minus 160 euros. See https://lichtersapps.com/ai/business-start.md
- **Video stayed part of my life.** Later I ran a self-improvement YouTube channel, and a fitness TikTok account I started in January 2025 had about 18,000 followers by January 2026.
- **Views and money are different goals.** In September 2026 I wrote that YouTube brings almost no money and that forcing yourself to post eats your focus. Views help most when they lead people to something you actually offer.
