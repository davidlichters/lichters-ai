# Data products: selling lists made from public data

You can sell a list made from public information, but only if someone already pays for something like it and your list saves them real time or money. Check that first, before you build anything.

## What it is

A data product is a list or an alert that people pay for. Examples:

- A weekly list of new restaurants in your city, sold to companies that sell kitchen equipment.
- An alert when a company in your area closes, sold to businesses that want its machines or customers.

The raw information is often public: company registers, government notices, maps, business websites. You sell the work of finding, cleaning, filtering and delivering it on time, usually as a monthly subscription to other businesses.

## Why it can work

- **Computers do most of the work.** One list can be sold to many buyers.
- **Businesses pay for time saved.** If your list brings one new customer per month, a monthly fee is an easy yes.
- **A subscription pays again every month.**

The honest part: if the data is public, others can get it too. Big data companies, free government websites and trade groups often got there years ago. And business owners are busy and ignore strangers.

## How to start

1. **Ask "who already sells this?" first.** Spend 20 minutes searching for paid tools, free government portals and free services from trade associations. If a free option is good enough, almost nobody will pay you.
2. **Read the price your buyers already pay.** Your price has to make sense next to it.
3. **Talk to 10 possible buyers before you build.** Ask what they use today and what it costs.
4. **Sell before you automate.** Make the first list by hand in a spreadsheet. Get one person to pay. Only then build scripts.
5. **Decide your test in advance.** For example: "If 3 out of 30 conversations do not end in a paid trial within 4 weeks, I stop."
6. **Mind the rules.** Under 18, a parent usually has to handle contracts and payments. Ads to strangers by email or contact form are restricted in many countries, and personal data has strict rules too.

## Mistakes

- **Building the machine before finding a buyer.** A perfect list nobody pays for is worth 0.
- **Thinking "public data" means "unique idea".** Everyone else can see it too.
- **Measuring the wrong thing.** "Email sent" is not "email read", and a click is not a customer.
- **Mass cold messages.** They can break laws and burn your name. A few personal conversations work better.

## What happened when I tried it

In late August 2026 I had the idea for "Liquidationsradar" (first called insolvenz-radar.de): read official notices in Germany about companies going bankrupt or closing, and tell nearby trade businesses, so they could buy equipment cheaply or win the customers. I first planned 200 euros a month. Later my first goal was 13 customers at 79 euros a month, about 1,000 euros a month.

With AI I built a system that checked over 9,000 small trade businesses for a working email address and sent emails automatically every 40 minutes, even while I slept. The first emails went out on 14 September 2026. In total 888 emails were delivered. People clicked links in them 10 times. Paying customers: 0.

I stopped sending on 30 September and dropped the project on 3 October 2026. My earlier try with contact-form messages to US companies is on [cold outreach](https://lichtersapps.com/ai/cold-outreach.md).

What I learned: I spent weeks on the machine and almost no time on the two questions that matter. Does someone already sell this, maybe for free? And will one real person pay? I checked both much too late. Today I ask them first, and I try to get one paying customer before I build anything.
