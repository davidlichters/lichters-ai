# Building iOS apps: small tools for people who already pay

With AI you can build a working iPhone app in days, so building is no longer the hard part. Finding a small group that already pays for a bad app, and getting found in App Store search, is.

## What it is

You make a small app that does one job for one group, like a score counter for darts players or a row counter for knitters. People find it by searching the App Store.

Apple keeps a cut of every sale, usually 15 percent for small developers, plus a yearly developer fee (around 99 dollars in many countries).

## Why it can work

- **Low cost.** No stock, no shipping.
- **People search for what they need.** Someone who types "darts scoreboard" already wants a darts app.

The honest part: this got much harder. Of 6,488 apps in my searches, about 30 percent were under a year old, and most of those had fewer than 20 ratings. The obvious "tracker for X" ideas are full. My rough estimate from public data: out of 100 new small apps, maybe 2 to 8 make 1,000 dollars a month after about two years.

## How to start

1. **Sort out the account first.** Apple needs a real adult as the account holder, with true name and address. Under 18, a parent has to own the account, the bank details and the tax side. Never use a fake age or someone else's name.
2. **Find demand before you build.** Good signs: the top apps have hundreds or thousands of ratings, but they are old, buggy, too expensive, or missing a key feature.
3. **Check that money exists.** My rule: only build if competitors clearly make at least around 2,000 dollars a month. Free check: a paid app with thousands of ratings means people pay.
4. **Build one small thing well.** One main job, done better than the old app.
5. **Pick a simple price and name.** A free version with one fair limit, then a one-time purchase or a yearly plan. Put the search words in the name, like "Ton80: Darts Scoreboard".

## Mistakes

- **Building first, checking later.** An app nobody searches for gets zero downloads.
- **Copy apps in bulk.** Apple rejects many near-identical apps from one developer (rule 4.3).
- **Fake reviews or tricky rating popups.** Only sending happy users to the App Store breaks Apple's rules. Use Apple's own rating dialog after a good moment.
- **Picking a niche with no money.** I dropped a beekeeping app because the leading apps made about 24 and 475 dollars a month.
- **Believing the estimate.** Revenue guesses can be off by a factor of 10. They are a filter, not a promise.

## What happened when I tried it

I started building iOS apps in October 2026 with AI (Claude Code, Xcode, SwiftUI). Before writing code I checked over 400 app ideas against App Store search, autocomplete, thousands of reviews and revenue estimates from a short AppTweak trial, and ranked 100 gaps by expected money.

In about ten days we built 14 apps from that list, for example Ton80 (darts scores) and Breedbook (dog breeder log).

The honest result so far: **zero apps in the App Store, zero downloads, zero euros.** Submitting is blocked by the boring adult parts: developer account details, an address, a registered business, bank and tax data. The plan is three apps a week after that, then a check after 60 days.

What I learned already: with AI, building is not the bottleneck. Choosing the niche and the paperwork are the real work.
