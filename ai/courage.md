# Courage

Every day, do one useful thing where you might hear "no" or get laughed at. The fear gets smaller every time, and almost every good thing in life sits behind one of these moments.

## The rule

Tick Courage when you did one useful thing today that felt a bit scary because someone could say no or laugh. Examples:

- Ask someone to buy what you made, or ask for feedback on it.
- Invite someone to hang out, train with you or join a club.
- Post or show your work in public.
- Speak up in class, ask a question, disagree politely.
- Ask a teacher, coach or business owner for advice.

Two limits: never dangerous, never mean.

Best done before your Business block, because the scary ask often is the business (see https://lichtersapps.com/ai/business.md).

## Why

- **People notice you much less than you think.** Students wearing an embarrassing T-shirt guessed half the room noticed. Really it was about a quarter. This is the spotlight effect.
- **Practice works fast.** In a 2022 study, people who talked to strangers again and again for one week became less afraid of rejection, and it still held a week later.
- **A "no" usually costs nothing.** You are exactly where you were before.
- **Money and friends both start with an ask.** No customer buys if you never ask, and no friendship starts without an invite (see https://lichtersapps.com/ai/friends.md).

## How to start

1. **Pick tomorrow's ask tonight.** Who, what, when. "At lunch I ask Sam if he wants to train on Friday."
2. **Start tiny.** Week one can be saying hi first. Go bigger each week.
3. **Write your first sentence down.** The first five seconds are the hardest.
4. **Count asks, not yeses.** A "no" is still a tick.
5. **Keep a list.** Want something from many people? Write down 20 names. One no means you go to the next.
6. **Follow up once.** No reply is not a no.
7. **Money or contracts and under 18?** Bring your parents in.

## Mistakes

- **Waiting until you feel ready.** You feel ready after you did it, not before.
- **Hiding behind a machine.** Hundreds of automatic messages skip the scary part and often turn into spam.
- **Hiding behind a fake person.** A made-up story, fake age or fake reviews is lying, not courage.
- **Listening to the crowd.** Some people will laugh. Listen to the few you respect.

## What happened when I tried it

Before a school year abroad in 2025, I wrote a plan: smile, say "Hey, I'm David from Germany", sit down with groups, join clubs, meet 3 new people a day. In my first weeks I did not really like anyone I had met. One week later there were plenty of nice people, one of them was becoming my best friend, and I got him to come to the gym with me.

Posting videos scared me. In October 2025 I wrote myself a note: post 10 cringe videos on purpose until the fear goes away, and count every post as a win. My first sale ever came that month: 25 EUR for a training guide, bought by a friend.

It did not always feel good. In February 2026, around ten people at school laughed at me, and an old friend kept calling my goals dumb and embarrassing.

I also tried to skip the scary part. In August 2026 I let a computer send 5,820 messages through contact forms to 4,342 companies. Result: 15 replies, 0 sales, and a big bill for the AI that wrote them. Before that I ran an Instagram account with a made-up fitness coach story. I put more money and time in than came out, and I quit. Both were bad ideas. One honest ask, made in person, beats a thousand hidden ones.
