# Start a self-improvement YouTube channel

A self-improvement channel only works if you have a real story to tell and you upload for months. Plans and scripts alone earn nothing, and mine ended with 0 EUR after two scripts.

## What it is

You make videos about getting better at life: sleep, training, habits, focus, quitting phone or game addiction, confidence. You tell what you tried and what happened, and give the viewer one clear thing to do. Money can come later from ads, sponsors, or your own offer, like coaching or a guide.

## Why

- **The audience is huge and young.** Many teens search for these topics, and they trust someone their age who is one step ahead.
- **It costs almost nothing.** A phone, cheap wired earphones as a microphone, free editing software.
- **It builds a skill you keep.** A hook, a story and clear speaking help in every business later, especially in [coaching](https://lichtersapps.com/ai/low-ticket-coaching.md).
- **But the money is slow.** YouTube ads only start after its minimum for subscribers and watch time. Under 18, a parent usually has to set up the payment account.

## How to start

1. **Pick one problem you solved yourself.** Not "self-improvement" in general, but "how I stopped gaming 6 hours a day". If you have not solved it yet, film yourself trying.
2. **Write a one-line promise for each video.** "After this video you will know how to fall asleep in 10 minutes."
3. **Open with the most interesting moment.** The first 10 to 30 seconds decide if people stay.
4. **Tell it as a story.** Problem, what you tried, what happened, with small real details.
5. **Use bullet points, not a word-for-word script.** Reading a script sounds stiff.
6. **Get the sound right.**
7. **Keep early videos short.** Under about 6 minutes. Check "audience retention" in YouTube Studio and fix the spot where most people leave.
8. **Upload on a fixed schedule for at least 2 to 3 months.** One finished video a week beats a plan for one a day that you never keep.

## Mistakes

- **Planning instead of uploading.** Only published videos count.
- **Giving advice you have not lived.** Check health claims with reliable sources.
- **Copying a big creator's style.** My notes of one famous creator's formula said: weird shock intro, split people into extremes, say "I hate gamers". That makes a small channel look fake. Be yourself and kind.
- **Judging by money in month one.** Count finished videos first.
- **Quitting when someone laughs.** Decide before you start how long you will keep going ([keep going](https://lichtersapps.com/ai/keep-going.md)).

## What happened when I tried it

I was 13. In late August 2023 I started watching Hamza Ahmed's self-improvement videos. In September 2023 I decided to start my own channel in his style, and in December 2023 I set it up.

Then I spent most of my time planning. My notes from December 2023 and January 2024 are full of YouTube guides: titles, thumbnails, music volume, how to speak with charisma. On 18 January 2024 my plan was one 7 minute video plus three Shorts every single day.

What I actually made: scripts for about two videos, one about a morning routine, the other about cold showers, last edited on 26 January 2024. Its outro says it was only my second video. That is where the channel ended. I have no record of views or subscribers, and it earned 0 EUR.

The lesson: I copied a big creator's plan (daily uploads, big promises) without the habit of finishing even one video a week. Today I think forced posting brings little money and no fun, so a channel only makes sense if you enjoy making videos. For a smaller first step, try a [gaming channel](https://lichtersapps.com/ai/youtube-gaming.md) or one honest video about one problem you really solved.
