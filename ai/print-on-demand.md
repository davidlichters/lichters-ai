# Print on demand: selling your own T-shirt designs

Print on demand lets you sell shirts without buying stock, but the printing is the easy part: the hard part is a design that a specific group of people really wants.

## What it is

You make a design (text, a drawing, a joke) and put it on a product in an online shop. When someone orders, a print company prints that one shirt and ships it. You never touch a shirt.

Well-known print companies are Printful, Printify, Gelato and Spreadshirt. Some have their own marketplace, others connect to your shop on Shopify or Etsy.

A made-up example: the print company charges about 13 dollars for a shirt plus 5 dollars shipping. You sell it for 28 dollars. That leaves about 10 dollars before shop fees, payment fees and ads. Count all of them before you set a price.

## Why it can work

- **Almost no starting cost.** No stock, no warehouse.
- **You can test fast.** A new design takes an hour, so you can try ten ideas in a week.
- **Trends are a real chance.** When everyone talks about a joke or a moment, a funny shirt about it can sell for a few weeks.

The honest part: millions of plain shirts already exist. "A shirt with a cool word on it" is not a business. Ask: what makes this shirt different from the ones in any shopping street?

## How to start

1. **Check the rules first.** Shops and print companies need an adult account holder. Under 18, a parent usually has to own the accounts and handle the money. Selling can count as a business, so check the rules in your country.
2. **Pick a group, not a product.** Fans of one small local club, people with one hobby, players of one game, dog owners of one breed. People buy shirts that say "this is me".
3. **Find out what already sells.** Search the group's name plus "shirt" on Etsy, Amazon and Google Shopping. Read the best sellers' reviews.
4. **Make 5 to 10 designs.** Free tools like Canva are enough.
5. **Order one sample to yourself.** Check print, size and how it feels after one wash. Real photos look better than fake mockups.
6. **Show it to the group.** Post photos where that group already is. Ask which design they would actually buy.
7. **Only pay for ads after free posts get real interest.** Set a small limit first.

## Mistakes

- **Using logos, brand names or famous people.** Team logos, cartoon characters and celebrity faces are owned by someone. It can get your shop shut down and cost money.
- **No group in mind.** A shirt "for everyone" is a shirt for no one.
- **Never ordering a sample.** Otherwise you learn about a blurry print from angry buyers.
- **Quitting after three days.** Decide before you start how long and how many designs your test will be.

## What happened when I tried it

Printed T-shirts were one of many things I tried in summer 2023, when I was 13. My notes have one line about it: I started selling printed T-shirts on 22 June 2023. The next line is a new idea, a bubblegum machine, dated 25 June. No sale is written down anywhere.

In my later list of everything I tried in 2023, my own summary says none of it worked, and all together it cost me about 160 euros or more.

Looking back, my notes show no target group, no sample order and no plan for how long to test. Just a date, and three days later the next idea. Print on demand was not the problem. Jumping to a new idea every few days was.
