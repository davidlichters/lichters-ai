# Reselling and local jobs: the best first business at 13

Selling old stuff and doing jobs for neighbors is boring, but it is the fastest honest way to make your first real money, because the customer already exists and already wants it.

## What it is

- **Reselling:** you sell things people no longer need. Start with your own old stuff (games, clothes, toys, books). Later you can buy cheap at flea markets and sell for more.
- **Local jobs:** you do a task people don't want to do. Mowing lawns, raking leaves, washing cars, walking dogs, shoveling snow.

No website, no ads, no followers.

## Why

- **Demand is already there.** People search eBay and local marketplaces every day for things like the ones in your basement. Your neighbor's grass grows every week.
- **You learn the real skills.** Pricing, talking to customers, keeping promises, handling a complaint.
- **Almost zero risk.** You don't buy stock or pay for ads before you sell.

## How to start

**Reselling**

1. **Collect.** Walk through your room, basement and garage. Ask your parents what can go.
2. **Check what it really sells for.** On eBay, filter for *sold* listings, not active ones. Asking prices are wishes, sold prices are facts.
3. **Do the money math before you list.** Price minus platform fee minus shipping minus packaging is what you keep. In many categories the fee alone is around 7 to 13 percent.
4. **Take good photos.** Daylight, plain background, 3 to 5 photos, show any damage honestly.
5. **Bundle the small stuff.** Ten cheap items in one lot sell faster than ten separate listings.
6. **Local pickup for big or cheap things.** Price a bit below the sold average so it actually goes.

Most selling platforms require you to be 18. If you're younger, a parent opens the account and gets paid, and you do the work.

**Local jobs**

1. **Pick one job** you can do with what your family already owns.
2. **Ask the closest people first.** Ring the bell or leave a short handwritten note with your name, the job, the price and a parent's phone number.
3. **Set a clear price per job.** "One lawn, 15 euros" is easy to say yes to.
5. **Ask for the next date right away.** One weekly lawn is worth more than ten one-time jobs.

Tell your parents where you're working, and only work for people they know.

## Mistakes

- **Pricing from active listings** instead of sold ones. Your stuff sits there for weeks.
- **Forgetting fees and shipping.** A sale that looks like 20 euros can leave you with 8.
- **Buying stock before you've sold anything.** Reinvest only what you earned.
- **Hiding damage.** One bad review costs more than the honest lower price.
- **Thinking it's beneath you.** The boring jobs are the ones that pay first.

## What happened when I tried it

In 2023, when I was 13, I tried around a dozen business models. One was **selling golf and tennis balls**, which I started on 3 July 2023. It didn't work.

All my 2023 experiments together came out at roughly **minus 160 euros**. In my own summary at the end of the year, only three things had actually worked: YouTube, **mowing lawns** and **eBay**. My note about eBay: it worked, but only if you have something old to sell. About all three: they work, but it's a lot of work. I didn't write down how much I earned.

That's the lesson. The boring local stuff brought in money. The exciting online ideas cost money. I went back to eBay later: in August 2026 I set out to sell over 100 items from our basement, local pickup only, priced below the sold average, small things bundled into lots.
