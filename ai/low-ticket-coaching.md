# Low-ticket coaching: selling a cheap guide you made yourself

A low-ticket product is a small digital thing, like a PDF guide, that you make once and sell many times for a low price. It only works if people already trust you before they see it.

## What it is

"Low ticket" means a cheap price, usually 5 to 50 euros. The product is digital: a PDF, a short video course or a training plan. Someone pays, gets a download link by email, done.

Example: you got strong in the gym, so you write a beginner plan as a PDF and sell it to people who watch your gym videos.

## Why it's a good first business

- **You make it once.** The tenth copy costs you nothing.
- **No shipping, no stock.**
- **You learn real skills:** writing words that sell, making videos and building trust.

The catch: a cheap product needs many buyers. At 10 euros you need 100 sales for 1,000 euros. Those buyers come from an audience, and building an audience is the slow part.

## How to start

1. **Teach one thing you actually did.** Like "how I went from beginner to my first pull-up". Real experience is your only advantage against free YouTube videos.
2. **Check who already sells it.** Find 5 guides on the same topic. Note price, title and the problem they promise to fix. Make yours more specific, not just cheaper.
3. **Build the audience first.** Post short videos about your topic for months.
4. **Ask before you build.** Post "would you want a beginner plan as a PDF?" and see who says yes.
5. **Make version one fast.** A simple, clean PDF with checklists is enough.
6. **Use a selling platform.** Services like Gumroad handle payment and the download. Most need an adult, so under 18, set it up with a parent.
7. **One clear sales page with a fair price.** Who it's for, what changes after reading it, what's inside. No fake countdowns, no fake reviews.
8. **Mention it in your videos** with one honest line, then measure views, clicks and sales.

## Mistakes

- **Building the product before the audience.** A great guide with no viewers sells nothing.
- **A fake high price with a fake "sale".** I did this: 149 euros crossed out, "83% Halloween sale" for 24.99. It looks like a trick. Show the real price.
- **Promising results you can't guarantee.** "Get abs in 30 days" is a lie for most people.
- **Selling something you never did.** I did that once, see https://lichtersapps.com/ai/tiktok-course.md, and it was a bad idea.
- **Giving risky advice.** For fitness, stick to safe beginner basics, no extreme diets, and tell people with health problems to ask a doctor.

## What happened when I tried it

- **18 January 2025:** my first fitness video on TikTok. I had grown from very skinny to one of the most muscular people in my class, so I wanted to teach beginners.
- **2025:** about a year improving the Gym Starter Guide, then on Gumroad with a list price of about 149 euros.
- **Launch day, October 2025:** 0 sales. I was sad and honestly scared.
- **31 October 2025:** first sale, 25 euros, through a Halloween offer of 24.99. The only guide sale I have written proof of.
- **16 November 2025:** I stopped the cheap guide and switched to 1:1 coaching for a higher price (https://lichtersapps.com/ai/high-ticket-coaching.md).
- **January 2026:** about 18,000 followers instead of my goal of 100,000, and no real growth for six months.

What I learned: the audience was too small and grew too slowly. A cheap product only works with a lot of people who already trust you. With a small audience, talking to people one by one works better. And I spent a year on the guide before a single person had said they would buy it.
